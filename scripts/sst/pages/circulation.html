How to read the panels
  • Map: potential vorticity on the 330 K isentrope; the black line is 2 PVU, so inside it is stratospheric air, the upper trough. Grey arrows: 200–850 hPa shear of the total wind; red: the storm's environmental shear (its own circulation removed). The IFS control (ECMWF's unperturbed 9 km run, called HRES until May 2026), 6-hourly, on its 0.25° grid and then on its 0.1° grid (interpolated from the model’s ~9 km grid) once that is out; the map's title gives the grid.
  • Hodograph: the environmental wind (storm's own circulation removed) at every level from 1000 to 100 hPa. Bold: the IFS control, coloured from low levels (warm) to high (blue); thin: other models; grey: the 50 members. Red dashed: the 200–850 hPa shear vector; black: storm motion. Storm-relative subtracts each run's own storm motion at that hour from its winds, the frame in which the low-level inflow into the storm is read; the 200–850 hPa shear is a difference between two levels, so it is the same in either frame. Under the hodograph and in the summary: the shear's direction against the storm's track (along it, against it, or how many degrees to its left or right).
  • Tilt: the 850→500 hPa vorticity-centroid displacement in the shear's frame, downshear up. Lines: each run's path to day 5, the dot at the chosen hour; grey dots: members; red diamonds: the observed 2–6 km tilt from NOAA's tail Doppler radar, rotated by the IFS control's shear within 3 hours of the radar time (radar analyses from before the run are left out here; the time series shows them).
  • Time series: the chosen measure along every run, with the AIFS-ENS member spread; the dark vertical line is the hour on the slider. Keyboard: the arrow keys step the slider once it has focus.
