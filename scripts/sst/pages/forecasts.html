Winter outlook — 2 m temperature, honestly re-based

The same seven C3S systems, turned into winter maps that avoid the two standard traps. First, the climatology trap: C3S anomalies are quoted against each model's own 1993–2016 hindcast — a climate centred on ~2004. Here each model's anomaly (bias and drift removed) is re-expressed against two observed bases with exact per-gridpoint ERA5 shifts: 1991–2020 (WMO standard) and 2016–2025 (the recent-decade normal used on energy desks, which absorbs the warming trend by construction — note how the central/southern U.S. flips cool against it while staying "warm" vs 1991–2020). Second, the equal-weight trap: every model's 24-year hindcast is verified against ERA5 and weighted ∝ 1/CRPS² per lead, so a model keeps its short-range credibility even where its long-range skill is noise. Tercile probabilities come from weighted member counting against ERA5 terciles — no Gaussian assumptions. December and January from the August issue; February joins with the September issue.

Winter snowfall extremes — risk of a top-10/5/1% month

Six of the eight systems publish snowfall (NCEP and BoM don't). Thresholds are each model's own hindcast percentiles per gridpoint and month — model-consistent by construction, no re-basing needed and no distribution assumed — and the maps show the member fraction exceeding them, averaged across models. Read against the neutral values (10 / 5 / 1%): the early signal is the El Niño duality — run-of-the-mill snow months suppressed across most of the continent, but the extreme tail (p99 — blockbuster months) enhanced along the subtropical-jet storm track from the southern Plains through the Midwest into the Northeast, where a moisture-loaded jet meets still-cold air. Fewer snow months, bigger blockbusters.

Sustained cold spells — the risk monthly means hide

Monthly-mean maps can't see a Uri: a brutal 6-day outbreak inside an otherwise mild month averages away. This uses the daily fields of every ensemble member (ECMWF SEAS5 ×51, ECCC GEM5-NEMO and CanESM5 ×20 each — 91 members) and asks, gridpoint by gridpoint: what fraction of members produce ≥5 consecutive days below normal −1σ (and a −2σ tier) between Dec 1 and the end of the integration? Each member is scored against a climatology built from its own model's daily hindcast per lead day — the only correction that survives the ~3 °C offset between the C3S monthly and daily streams — with normal and σ from ERA5 1991–2020 dailies. The middle column is the honesty check: the identical spell test run on the 30 observed winters. The difference map is the forecast: cold-spell risk suppressed nearly continent-wide under the record El Niño — except a coherent patch over Texas and the southern Plains sitting above its already-nontrivial base rate. A warm winter with a loaded southern cold-outbreak gun is exactly the strong-Niño precedent (Feb 2021 was a La Niña, but Jan 2010 and Dec 2015's southern outbreaks were not).

Permian freeze-off risk — absolute thresholds, not anomalies

Gas production doesn't care about standard deviations — wellheads freeze at fixed temperatures. Same daily members, bias-corrected into absolute °C, scored against operational thresholds over the producing basins: ≥3 and ≥5 consecutive days below 0 °C daily mean (the 5-day run is the Uri class — Feb 2021 gave Midland ~7) and any day below −5 °C. At the Midland gridpoint (★) the members currently give a 53% chance of a ≥3-day freeze, 20% for the Uri-class ≥5-day run, and 23% for at least one hard-freeze day — consistent with the cold-spell map: the one region where this warm winter's tail risk is fat.

Does the cold-spell product actually work? A 24-winter backtest

Before trusting the maps above, the same machinery was run on the 24 hindcast winters (1993/94–2016/17, August issues — the same issue month), leave-one-out: each winter is scored against a climatology built from the other 23 years, so no winter ever grades its own homework, and truth is the identical spell test on ERA5. Three findings. The pattern is skillful: multi-model median Brier skill +0.13 over climatology with 84% of the scored area positive — and combining the three models beats each alone (+0.09–0.11). The probabilities mean what they say: the reliability curve tracks the diagonal from 5% to 75% — when the product says 40%, it happens about 40% of the time. But respect the gridpoint limit: at Midland specifically, year-to-year discrimination of ≥3-day freezes is absent (BSS −0.17; the members hover near the 33% base rate every winter), and no hindcast winter produced a ≥5-day Uri-class run there — the live 20% is a genuine tail with no in-sample precedent. Read the regional pattern, which verifies; treat any single gridpoint's number as climatology-plus-pattern, not a point forecast. Hindcast ensembles are half the real-time size, so these scores understate the operational product.
