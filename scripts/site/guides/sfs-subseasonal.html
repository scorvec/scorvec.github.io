The page

NOAA's Seasonal Forecast System — the coupled UFS successor to CFSv2, still experimental (beta2) — runs 31 members from the first of each month. Anomalies are against its own 1991–2020 reforecast for the same start month and lead, so the model's drift is not read as a forecast. Weekly maps and terciles for weeks 1–6 and the daily animation, in the same layout as the GEFS and GEPS pages, and how its hindcasts score against those two. An issue appears about five days after the 1st, so by mid-month its first one or two weeks are already past. Its monthly and seasonal forecast is on the seasonal page.

On the first of each month NOAA's SFS beta2 runs 31 members to day 46; NOAA publishes them about five days later. Each field is regridded to the GEFS page's 1.5° grid and every forecast day is differenced from the mean of SFS's own reforecast at the same lead — 11 members from the same start date of every year 1991–2020, which removes the model's drift and its mean bias together; because the reforecast spans the normal period itself, no further re-basing is needed. 500 hPa height has its global mean removed so the circulation shows. The tercile maps count the 31 members against the terciles of the reforecast's own weekly anomalies (330 per gridpoint and week), so their normal is 1991–2020, not the 2016–2025 terciles of the GEFS and GEPS pages: part of any “above normal” here is the warming since then. The maps are dotted wherever the reforecast ensemble mean's anomaly correlation with ERA5, over the starts of the same month with a leave-one-year-out climatology, is significantly above zero (one-sided 5 % test, false-discovery rate 10 % across the map): temperature worldwide on the 1991–2025 starts, precipitation over the Northern Hemisphere on 1991–2023, the extent of the site's ERA5 precipitation store. NOAA's own published climatology files are not used: the October one was found to be a copy of September's. The hindcast comparison scores SFS, GEFSv12 and GEPS on 38 starts (1 September and 1 October, 2001–2019; for GEFSv12 the Wednesday start within 3 days of the 1st) as four-member means against ERA5.

The figures

Weekly means

Seven-day means of the 31-member ensemble mean, anomaly against SFS’s own 1991–2020 reforecast at the same lead. With the skill layer on, cells are dotted where the reforecast’s anomaly correlation with ERA5 for this week and start month is significantly above zero (one-sided 5 % test on 35 years for temperature, 33 for precipitation; false-discovery rate 10 % across the map), and cross-hatched where there is no ERA5 truth.

Weekly terciles

The most likely tercile by week: colour is the category (blue below, grey near, red above normal), intensity its probability, from the fraction of the 31 members in each tercile of the reforecast’s own 1991–2020 weekly anomalies. The GEFS and GEPS pages use 2016–2025 terciles, so part of any “above” here is the warming since then. Dots mark reforecast skill significantly above zero (one-sided 5 % test on 35 years for temperature, 33 for precipitation; false-discovery rate 10 %); the probabilities are shown everywhere. For precipitation, cross-hatching marks cells with no truth (the site’s ERA5 store holds Northern Hemisphere precipitation only, to 2023) and dry climatological weeks.

Daily animation

One frame per forecast day, nine fields: the ensemble mean of the day, anomaly against the lead-matched SFS reforecast.

Hindcast skill

Two comparisons, each read in the caption under the figure, which is rewritten from the scores whenever they are rebuilt. Against GEFS and GEPS: SFS beta2, GEFSv12 and GEPS hindcasts on 38 starts (1 September and 1 October, 2001–2019; for GEFSv12 the Wednesday start within 3 days of the 1st), each a four-member mean against its own hindcast climatology, verified against ERA5, for three fields in two regions. Against CFSv2, the system SFS is meant to replace: 1 September and 1 October starts 1999–2025 (no 2017), each against its own hindcast climatology, verified against ERA5. “Significant” means p < 0.05 with a false discovery rate of 10 % over the figure’s comparisons; ▲ marks the other system ahead, ▼ SFS ahead. The seasonal comparison (Niño-3.4, three-month temperature and precipitation) is on the seasonal page.

Daily data check

Every issue is checked before anything is drawn: all forecast days filled, the 35–65°N temperature trend across the forecast close to the reforecast’s for the same start month, and the daily fields averaged over the first month matching the issue’s own monthly mean. An issue that fails is held back and this view says which test it failed; the weekly maps, terciles and animation appear by themselves once NOAA’s daily store passes. An issue whose daily file was mislabelled but whose first days could be recovered is drawn for those days only, with the check described on the page.
