The page

Integrated vapour transport (IVT), the moisture carried sideways through the column, is the variable atmospheric rivers are defined by. It is computed here for each model from its humidity and wind levels. The page runs from the four deterministic models to day 7, through four ensembles to day 15, to the GEFS extended run in weeks 3–5, where only differences from the model’s own climate that pass a significance test are drawn.

IVT maps, days 1–7

Integrated vapour transport

Each map is the model’s own IVT, (1/g) |∫ q V dp| from 1000 to 300 hPa, integrated with the trapezoid rule over the pressure levels the model publishes. Where the ground is higher than a level, that level is left out and the lowest layer starts at the surface pressure. Without this step, the extrapolated values that pressure-level files carry below the Sierra Nevada, the Cascades and the Rockies would add transport that is not there. The 1000 hPa cap is the same for every model, so over the ocean the thin layer below 1000 hPa is left out by all four alike.

Levels used. AIFS and IFS: 1000, 925, 850, 700, 600, 500, 400 and 300 hPa (the open data has no others in that range); AIFS 0.25°; the IFS control 0.25° when its run first comes out, then on the 0.1° grid (interpolated from the model’s ~9 km grid), redrawn from that run about two hours later, once ECMWF publishes it (each frame's subtitle gives the grid; the North Pacific view shades it in 0.2° blocks, since a 0.1° cell is smaller than a pixel there). GFS: all 17 levels from 1000 to 300 (every 25–50 hPa), 0.25°. GDPS: 19 levels (1000, 985, 970, 950, 925, 900, 875, 850, 800 … 300), 15 km latitude–longitude grid. The level-set bias is measured every run: … Shading starts at 100 in two greys for transport below the AR threshold. From 250, the colours split each step of the Ralph AR scale in two. Arrows show the IVT vector where it is 250 or more; contours are sea-level pressure every 4 hPa. The North Pacific view covers the West Coast landfalls; the North America view covers Gulf and Atlantic moisture plumes into the South and East.

West Coast landfall, days 1–7

Coastline IVT

The landfall view of the same IVT: latitude along the coast against time, read at the first ocean point west of the coastline on every 0.25° of latitude from Baja California to southeast Alaska. This is the same transect the ensemble charts use. Black contours mark 250, 500, 750 and 1000. A river that stalls shows as a horizontal band; one sweeping down the coast shows as a diagonal.

AR scale at the coast

The AR scale of Ralph et al. (2019, Bull. Amer. Meteor. Soc. 100, 269–289) at eleven points 2.5° apart from 30 to 55°N on the coastal transect, from the chosen model’s 6-hourly IVT. The table gives the strongest spell in the 7 days, its peak IVT, how long it stays at or above 250, when it starts, and the rank of the spell under way on each day. A spell still going at +168 h is cut off there, so its duration, and possibly its rank, can rise in later runs. The rank comes from the computed IVT, not from CW3E’s own landfall tool, so values can differ from theirs. Moisture around a tropical cyclone or its remnants also reaches these values (the San Diego and Baja points in early October 2026); the scale was designed for atmospheric rivers, so read such a rank as transport strength, not as an AR category.

AR probabilities, days 1–15

Chance of AR conditions

The share of members with IVT at or above 250 (AR conditions) or 500 (AR2 or stronger), 6-hourly to day 15, for each ensemble and for their equal-weight blend. The blend averages each system’s own member share, so a 21-member ensemble counts the same as a 51-member one. It uses the valid times every system covers and leaves out any system whose run is more than 24 hours older than the newest.

Why IVT is estimated, not computed, for the members. Exact IVT needs humidity and wind on every level for every member: 6–50 GB per ensemble per run. Each system therefore uses the estimate IVT ≈ k·W·|V850|, where W is total column water (ECMWF) or precipitable water (GEFS, GEPS) and V850 is the 850 hPa wind. k is refitted every run, through the origin, against that system’s own exact IVT for one member: the AIFS-ENS control on the 8 open-data levels; IFS-ENS member 1 (the open data have no IFS-ENS control at these steps); the GEFS control from relative humidity, temperature and wind on every level from 1000 to 300 in its two file sets; and the GEPS control, which has humidity only at 1000, 925, 850, 700, 500 and 250 hPa, so q at 600, 400 and 300 is interpolated log-linearly in pressure (fitted every 48 h of the run). The fit, its correlation, its error and how well the estimate hits the 250 threshold are listed under the AR-scale table. The estimate puts the vector along the 850 hPa wind, and it misses rivers whose moisture rides above 700 hPa.

Steps are 6-hourly everywhere: every system publishes 6-hourly or finer out to 360 h, and the finer early steps are not used, so the systems share one time axis and no step is interpolated.

Landfall odds along the coast

The share of members at or above 250 along the coastal transect, with the ensemble-mean IVT contoured at 250 and 500. A dashed line marks where half the members reach 500. The shading is the raw share of members (uncalibrated, not a verified probability) and the contours give the typical strength; individual members are in the plume view.

Member plumes at the coast

Every member’s estimated IVT at the chosen coastal point, 6-hourly to day 15, with the ensemble median and the 10–90 % band. Reference lines mark the AR-scale steps at 250, 500, 750 and 1000. The estimate is calibrated per system per run; see the ensemble maps for how.

AR-scale odds by day

For each member, every AR spell at the point is rated on the Ralph scale over its whole length within the forecast, and its rank is credited to every UTC day the spell touches. The table shows the share of members with an AR1-or-stronger (or AR3-or-stronger) spell on each day. The blend averages the systems equally, on the days every system covers in full. Spells cut off by the start or end of a forecast are rated on the part inside it.

Weeks 3–5

AR frequency against the model's climate

What is shown. AR frequency is the share of a week’s 28 six-hourly times with estimated IVT ≥ 250. Here it comes from the 31-member GEFS 00 UTC extended run and is shown as the difference from GEFS’s own reforecast climate at the same lead. That climate is the GEFSv12 reforecast 2000–2019: Wednesday 35-day starts within ±15 days of this run’s date, the control and four perturbed members. Comparing against the model’s own reforecast removes its moisture bias and its drift with lead. The estimate uses one fixed k for the live run and the reforecast alike, so the threshold means the same on both sides. Grey contours give the climatological frequency for context. Only 20°N and north is tested and shown: in the deep tropics IVT reaches 250 most of the time in the trade-wind moisture flux, which is not an atmospheric river.

Significance. Each 1° cell compares the 31 member frequencies with the reforecast’s member frequencies using a two-sided Mann–Whitney U test. The Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery rate is then held at 10 % across the map (Wilks 2016, Bull. Amer. Meteor. Soc. 97, 2263–2273). Cells that fail are left blank, and a week where nothing passes says so. The test treats the reforecast samples (about 85 starts × 5 members) as independent; members of one start, and starts a week apart, are correlated, so the p-values run slightly low. Passing means the forecast differs from the model’s climate. It is not evidence of skill: AR occurrence forecasts beyond two weeks have low skill over the western US (DeFlorio et al. 2019, J. Geophys. Res. Atmos. 124, 11242–11265), with occasional windows tied to the MJO and QBO. Two biases are uncorrected: the reforecast base period (2000–2019) is drier than today’s climate, and GEFS operations drift differently from the reforecast at long lead (see the GEFS page).

GEPS is not shown here: the GEPS reforecast the site holds has no moisture fields, so its weeks 3–5 cannot be tested against its own climate.

Moisture

Precipitable water

Ensemble-mean total column water (vapour plus cloud) and its anomaly against ERA5 1991–2020 for the same date (three annual harmonics on a 1.5° grid, fitted at 00 and 12 UTC; the 06 and 18 UTC frames are read against 12 and 00 UTC), 6-hourly to day 15, for AIFS-ENS and IFS-ENS. Precipitable water shows where the moisture is; IVT shows where it is moving. An ensemble mean blurs individual plumes in week 2. The anomaly is taken against ERA5, not the model’s own climate, so it also carries each model’s moist or dry bias, which can grow with lead.
