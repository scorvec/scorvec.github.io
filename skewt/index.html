Global sounding explorer, live and archived

Every radiosonde launch on Earth — live, and back more than a century. Click a station for the full analysis: parcels, ECAPE, shear, hodograph and where each value ranks in that station's own climatology, computed in your browser. Read the methodology first →  ·  US sounding gap report →

How to use it

Find a station by typing a city, WMO number (72357), ICAO code (KOUN) or country in the box above the map — or press / — and the stations you open stay one click away under “Recent”. In the sounding card the skew-T takes the left; on the right are the hodograph, a strip of key numbers (CAPE, shear, helicity, composites, PWAT, freezing level; amber and red mark notable values) and the full index tables, which scroll.

Blue stations reported at the last 00Z/12Z interval; purple ones launched off-hour (06Z, 15Z, 18Z…) — usually a special release ahead of severe weather; teal are active but missed the last slot, red are closed (archive only). Click a station, then pick Latest or Archive. The launch strip under the station name lists every release the mirror holds for the last four days, specials included — click one to open it, or step through launches with ◀ ▶. Recent launches load instantly; the first click on a station can take a few seconds, or longer for archived dates (older soundings come from NOAA's global archive as one per-station file, with a pulsing note showing progress) — then every other date at that station is instant. The analysis runs entirely in your browser via SHARPlib compiled to WebAssembly.

Data: latest soundings from the University of Wyoming archive (BUFR, mirrored hourly — UW sends no CORS headers); any historical date directly from NOAA IGRA v2 (first load per station can be tens of MB, then cached). Analysis: SHARPlib (K. Halbert, NWS SPC lineage) as WebAssembly — SB/ML/MU parcels, LI, DCAPE, effective inflow, Bunkers, SRH, effective shear, SCP/STP.
