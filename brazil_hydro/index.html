Brazil Hydro — ONS/SIN monitor

Sibling of the Colombia hydro project, built on ONS open data: ENA (natural inflow energy) and EAR (stored energy) daily by basin, 2000–present, with ONS's own long-term-mean (MLT) norms. Rain products, per-basin kernel models and AIFS+IFS ensemble fans follow the Colombia architecture and are under construction. This page is unlinked and provisional.

Ground truth — what biases does IMERG have over Brazil?

502 INMET automatic stations, 740 paired days. The verdict is far milder than Colombia's terrain extremes: overall factor ×0.93, with a coherent geography — IMERG runs wet over the interior highlands (São Francisco ×0.80, Tocantins ×0.84, Paranaíba ×0.85 — the classic dry-environment over-detection over the cerrado/sertão) and slightly dry along the coastal Atlantic serras (the orographic underestimate), while the SE/S basins are essentially unbiased (Tietê ×1.00, Jacuí ×1.01). Held-out validation: 14% median error reduction, 61% of stations improved — smaller gains than Colombia because the raw product is simply better here (raw station error ×1.17 vs Colombia's ×2.04). The correction field drives the model truth and the NWP verification; basin correlations are unchanged (bias cancels in r) but forecast levels and %-of-normal maps now sit on gauge-anchored ground.

Rain → ENA models + ensemble forecasts

Each basin's IMERG rainfall through fitted memory kernels (τ up to 120 days for the continental basins — São Francisco carries a 90-day memory) plus the EAR storage state, targeting ENA as % of MLT. The orange fans are ~101 bias-corrected AIFS-ENS + IFS-ENS members; bias factors and blend weights are measured from a 3-week verification archive (4,700+ pairs) and update twice daily. Paraná mainstem is structurally weak (routed upstream water + Itaipu regulation, not local rain).
