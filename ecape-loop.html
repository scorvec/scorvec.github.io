What you are looking at

CAPE assumes an updraft that never mixes with its surroundings — a sealed parcel rising untouched through the environment. Real updrafts entrain, pulling in environmental air through their flanks, and if that air is drier or cooler than the parcel then the mixture is less buoyant than the parcel was. ECAPE (Peters et al. 2023) is the version of CAPE that accounts for the mixing, derived from the storm-relative wind and shear as well as the thermodynamic profile — because the kinematics are what set how wide an updraft can be and how much it must ingest.

Every frame is the full HRRR 3 km grid: 1,905,141 columns, each one a sounding run through SHARPlib — the same code the Skew-T explorer runs in your browser, so a point here and a click there come from the same routine. The loop is the 48-hour forecast from one of the extended cycles (00/06/12/18 UTC are the only ones that run that far), hourly through F18 and 3-hourly to F48.

Reading the ratio — and why it exceeds 1

The two ratio fields are the reason this exists. Two places with identical CAPE can hand an updraft very different amounts of usable buoyancy, and no CAPE map can show you which is which.

RatioWhat it means
0.2–0.4Entrainment-hostile. Plenty of CAPE on paper, little of it usable — typically weak storm-relative flow, so updrafts stay narrow and dilute quickly.
0.5–0.7The common case; the CONUS-wide mean sits near 0.56.
0.8–1.0Efficient — wide updrafts, moist surroundings, or inflow strong enough that mixing costs little.
above 1.0Not an error. The formulation nets a storm-relative kinetic-energy gain against the entrainment loss, so where inflow is vigorous enough it more than pays for the mixing. Far from rare: the 99th percentile of the ratio runs near 1.4, and the high values organise along sheared corridors rather than scattering as noise.

The ratio is masked below 100 J kg⁻¹ of CAPE, where it is two near-zero numbers divided and carries no signal.

Caveats

These are model soundings. They inherit whatever the HRRR gets wrong about the boundary layer and low-level moisture, and ECAPE’s sensitivity to that moisture means a bias shows up amplified in the ratio. The updraft-width assumption inside the formulation is a parameterisation, not a measurement. And high ECAPE describes available buoyancy only — there is no forcing, no convergence and no cap strength in these panels, so it says nothing about whether anything will actually initiate.
