        About Claude’s call

        The forecast on this site gives every race, and each chamber, a probability. Only one election actually happens. Claude’s call is that one election, named in advance: a winner and a margin in every race, so the seat counts above are what the call adds up to.

        Why it is not just the favourite in every race

        Picking the forecast’s favourite everywhere gives a map no single election night produces. Polling errors move races together: if a party underperforms its polls in one state, it usually underperforms them in its neighbours too. So the call starts from one national picture, the size and direction of the overall polling miss and the national House vote, and calls each race inside that picture. A night that is a point worse for Democrats than their polls flips the close races that lean their way together, not a scattering of them at random. Races called against the forecast’s favourite are outlined in amber on the maps and marked in the tables.

        What it is built from

        The forecast’s final run is the starting point: its odds and margins for every race, at the national vote it expected. On top of that is judgement the forecast does not encode: how the final polls compare with how pollsters missed in recent elections, late movement, and what the early vote does and does not tell us. The reason for each call that leaves the forecast is given beside it. The margins are the call’s point estimates; the forecast’s chance for each called winner shows how likely the forecast thought that outcome was.

        How it is scored

        Each call is scored once its race is called on the results page: it is right if the called winner won. The margins are compared with the latest count, which stays provisional until the state certifies, beside the forecast’s median margin on the same races. The seat totals so far and the projections for the races still out come from the results page and from the races still out.

        When it was made

        The call was made once, the day before the election, against the forecast’s latest run (both times are at the top of the page). It is not updated for later polls or during the count. It is Claude’s own call, made by Anthropic’s Claude for this site; it is not a projection of results. In Maine and Alaska, where the count runs ranked-choice rounds, the call names the winner after the final round, and Alaska’s count is not complete until about November 18.
