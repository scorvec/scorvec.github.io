        About this page

        The results

        Calls and vote counts come from Wikipedia's 2026 election pages (text available under CC BY-SA 4.0), read every hour. A race counts as called once its winner is marked there. Counts are added as editors fill in each race's results table, or from civicAPI's House and Senate tallies when those have more votes counted. Until a state certifies, every margin on this page is the latest count and is marked provisional. Certified official returns replace these numbers as states certify, and from then on the official figures are used. A close race whose results table is still empty can take its count from news reports of the unofficial returns instead, with the share of the expected vote counted: Mississippi's Senate race took the AP count as reported in the press, with the share of the expected vote counted reported alongside it; a newer count keeps that share, scaled by the votes added since. A race a news organisation has called before Wikipedia marks it is entered by hand as called, with the organisation named.

        The projections under each chamber come from the races still out: the forecast's own simulations, updated with every call and count so far.

        The national House vote

        The margin is the Democratic lead as a share of the two parties' House votes, (D − R) / (D + R), the measure the forecast uses. Leads quoted as shares of every vote run a few tenths of a point smaller. Past elections are the House totals on Wikipedia's election pages. The projection is the median national vote in the forecast's simulations, reweighted by every call and count so far (the races still out). The counted tally is Wikipedia's national results table once editors fill it in, and civicAPI's national House tally until then. Until every state has counted it runs behind the final vote, and late ballots have run Democratic in recent elections, so the shifts use the median outcome until the count is final. Seats with only one party on the ballot add no votes for the other, which pulls the national vote a little away from the generic ballot.

        The popular vote at the top is every House vote counted so far, by party, from the national House tally of civicAPI, a free election-results service, read with every update. Parties with less than a twentieth of a percent of the vote are summed as everyone else. It is a count, not a projection: states that count ballots for days or weeks after Election Day (California above all) are not fully in yet, so it runs behind the final vote. Seats with only one party on the ballot add no votes for the other.

        What is scored

        The forecast gave each race a chance that the Republican's opponent (a Democrat, or an independent where one led the challenge) wins, and a range for the margin. The chances are scored on every called race: how often the favourite won, the Brier score (the mean squared gap between the chance and what happened; 0 is perfect, and always saying 50 % scores 0.25) and, for House seats, the same score for the race raters' average on the same seats. Margins are scored only once a count is final or certified. The figures above show provisional margins as hollow dots and final ones as solid dots. In the calibration chart, each group's bar is a 95 % interval for its share won, given how few races it holds.
