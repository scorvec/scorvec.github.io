    About this page

    What the projection is

    The final forecast simulated the election 20,000 times. Each simulation shares national, state, urban and demographic-group swings across every race. This page gives each simulation a weight for how well it matches the races called so far and every count so far, and reads the seat totals and race chances from the weighted simulations. A surprise in one race therefore moves every race that shares its swings. A called race counts for its winner in every seat total. In the weights, a call counts heavily against simulations where the called winner lost by more than a few points, rather than ruling them out. With hundreds of calls, the weights would otherwise rest on a handful of simulations, so their combined pull is softened just enough to keep about 2,000 effective simulations.

    The counts

    A count is taken to differ from the final margin by an error with heavy tails, so that one odd count cannot take over. A race that is still out and has a count of its own gets its chance by combining what every other call and count implies for it with its own count, read directly. Once at least 95 % of its vote is counted (a reported share, not an estimate), its own count alone sets its chance: the other races say nothing about which of its ballots are still out. When the share of the vote counted is reported (official returns, or a news count with its estimate of the expected vote), the error comes from the vote still out. The uncounted ballots are expected to vote like the ballots the race has counted since its first reported count, weighted by how many of those there are (with none yet, like every ballot counted so far), and may run about 25 points apart from that, so a count with 4 % of the vote left is good to about a point. A complete count is taken to be good to 0.1 points: the 36 statewide recounts from 2000 to 2023 moved the margin by 0.03 points on average and 0.11 at most (FairVote). Wikipedia's results tables and civicAPI's counts do not say how much of the vote is counted (civicAPI's precincts reporting reads 100 % in states still counting mail ballots), so for those counts it is estimated from the district's or state's 2024 presidential vote, scaled to a midterm turnout of 80 % of it (2018 ran about 90 % and 2022 about 71 %). The estimate is never allowed above 95 %, and because it can be well off, such a count's error never falls below 2 points. Arizona, Alaska, California, Colorado, Nevada, Oregon, Utah and Washington count most ballots after Election Day, and their late ballots can run well apart from the early count, so their counts carry extra uncertainty. Once states publish official returns with the share counted, those figures are used instead of the estimate.

    The popular vote

    The House popular vote at the top is every vote counted so far for a House candidate, by party, unofficial until states certify, from the national House tally of civicAPI, a free election-results service, read with every update. Parties with less than a twentieth of a percent of the vote are summed as everyone else. It is a count, not a projection: states that count ballots for days or weeks after Election Day (California above all) are not fully in yet, so it runs behind the final vote. Seats with only one party on the ballot add no votes for the other.

    Sources and limits

    Calls come from Wikipedia's 2026 election pages (text available under CC BY-SA 4.0), read every hour; see the results page. Counts come from Wikipedia or from civicAPI's House and Senate tallies, whichever has more votes counted (where both stood at the same point they matched to a vote or two). Wikipedia marks winners some hours after the news organisations call them, so many races listed here as still out may already be called elsewhere. For those, the chance shown is what the forecast and every other call imply. Margins are the Democrat's (or independent's) share of the vote minus the Republican's. The simulations come from the final forecast's own frozen code and data (commit in the model repository), so before any call or count they reproduce the forecast within simulation noise of a few tenths of a point. This page is automated and unreviewed, and it is not a race call.
