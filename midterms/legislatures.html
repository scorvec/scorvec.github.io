State legislatures, 2026

← Congress and governors

Chances of control of seventeen competitive state legislative chambers.

Experimental. This part of the forecast is new and rests on less evidence than the Congress and governor forecasts. Tested on 2018 and 2022 with only what was known on October 1, on the fourteen original chambers (24 chamber elections): the control chances scored a Brier score of 0.118, against 0.250 for simply assuming the current majority holds; the winner was called right in 79% of them against 75% for that naive rule, and the final seat count fell inside the 80% range in 23 of 24. The Georgia, Iowa and Texas Houses were tested separately (5 chamber elections with complete district data; Georgia 2018 lacked two districts): all five called right, as the naive rule also did, Brier 0.006, seat counts inside the 80% range 5 of 5 - a test with no close chamber in it. Known gaps:
  • New Hampshire House (400 seats): no candidate lists are available, so every seat is treated as contested by full slates of both parties, and floterial districts are simulated as ordinary multi-member districts; it was not part of the 2018 test.
  • Michigan Senate: no 2024 Senate election, and the 2026 court-ordered redraw of the Detroit-area districts is not yet in the Census boundary files; district leans are 2020 results moved by the 2024 swing, on the 2022 lines.
  • Minnesota Senate (and part of the New Hampshire Senate): no candidate lists; sitting senators are assumed to run unless they have announced their retirement.
  • Iowa House: candidates are described in prose; retirements are read from it, but most nominees are not, so a seat counts as contested unless the page says a party has no candidate.
  • Georgia and Texas Houses: many seats have only one party on the ballot; a seat is fixed only when the chamber page shows it (a general-election listing without the other party), otherwise it is simulated as contested.

How it works: each seat's expected margin is the district's 2024 presidential lean (precinct returns joined to legislative districts), the national environment from the generic ballot and incumbency, with errors that move every seat in a state together and with the national mood. (The state's own Senate and governor polls were also tested as a signal; they did not improve the backtest and are not used.) Multi-member districts elect their top candidates.
