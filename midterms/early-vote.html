Why the early-vote mix is not in the forecast

Whether a party's registrants are over- or under-represented among early ballots is the most quoted early-vote reading. It would enter the forecast only if it had foretold the polls' misses. North Carolina has the longest public record: the table scores the change in that reading from the previous cycle of the same kind against the state's polling miss (result minus the polls of the last three weeks, statewide races).

Method and sources

Reading the North Carolina numbers with care. Registration is not a vote: unaffiliated voters are the largest group on North Carolina's rolls and registered Democrats and Republicans both cross over. Who votes early also depends on habit and on what each party tells its voters: Republicans moved strongly toward early voting in 2024, and if more of them wait for Election Day this year their share of the early vote will understate them. The first days of early voting are a small part of the total and the busiest for the most habitual voters. The comparison with 2022 is the cleanest: a midterm with the same 17-day early-voting calendar starting on the third Thursday before the election.

North Carolina. The State Board of Elections' public files (dl.ncsbe.gov): each election's absentee file (one row per mail or one-stop ballot with the voter's county, party, race, age, ballot type, return date and status), the registration statistics on each earlier election day, this week's statewide registration file for 2026, voter history, and the 2024 precinct results for each county's presidential margin. A ballot counts once it is accepted, once per voter, on the day it came back; the last complete day is the day before the file was written. Earlier years come from their final files, which keep each ballot's return date. Only counts are kept: no voter's row, name or address is stored or published (midterms/nc_tracker.py).

Georgia. Counts compiled by georgiavotes.com from the Secretary of State's absentee voter files, read once a day; 2022 at the same point from the Internet Archive's captures of the same site (midterms/ga_early.py). Florida. The Division of Elections' daily mail-ballot and early-voting statistics files, by party, against month-end active registration and against 2024 at the same days out from the Internet Archive's captures (midterms/fl_early.py). Nevada. Clark County only, about 70% of the state's voters: the Secretary of State's statewide files turn away automated requests, the Clark County Election Department's do not. Its nightly mail ballot return file (each returned ballot with the voter's registered party and the day it came back; the 2022 and 2024 files the same way, for the same days out) and its daily in-person early vote files, joined on the voter id to the county's active voter list for party (a general election's file carries none; voters registered since the list was written are left unmatched). 2024's in-person voters are compared on turnout only. The county's daily files are unaudited (corrections reach its later cumulative files), and its last day is left out until a night's file holds all of it. Only counts are kept (midterms/nv_clark_early.py). Every state. How much of each state's vote is already in comes from the UF Election Lab (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), used only to compute each state's factor above; its counts are not republished here.

Texas. Turnout only: Texas has no party registration and publishes no party data, so no party split is estimated. The Secretary of State's early-voting turnout files (Civix portal), one per early-voting day with each county's registered voters, in-person early votes and mail ballots received, as the counties report them; a county that has not sent the day's file shows the day before's total. Early votes are in person plus mail received, as a share of the county's registered voters. Mail voting in Texas is limited (65 or older, disability, out of the county, confinement), so mail is a small and old-skewed slice. The county groups are fixed in advance from the 2024 presidential vote (MIT Election Data and Science Lab precinct returns by county), not chosen from the turnout. Early voting runs October 19 to 30, the same 12 days before Election Day as in 2022, so the two years line up day for day once 2022's final county early vote is added (midterms/tx_early.py).
