Race polls

How the polls are used

  1. Checked. Only polls naming the candidates actually on the ballot. The same poll reported by two sources is kept once.
  2. Corrected for the pollster's measured lean (or its campaign sponsor's), and for the state's persistent polling miss in the last two elections.
  3. Weighted by precision: fewer undecideds, no strong third candidate, an experienced pollster and a recent field date all count for more. Campaign polls count for 0.58.
  4. Blended with the race's fundamentals. A poll far from the others is partly discounted as a likely outlier, and every race keeps a shared polling-miss floor.

National polls. The generic ballot and presidential approval are each fitted as one smooth trend through every poll, with each pollster's lean and the gap between likely voters, registered voters and all adults estimated at the same time. A poll's adjusted value is what it says once its pollster's lean (lean fix) is taken out, and the gap between its population (likely voters, registered voters or adults) and the trend's, plus for a partisan or internal poll the lean such polls share (population & sponsor fix). Weight is what a poll brings into the fit: the square root of its sample size (100–1,500), scaled down when that pollster's polls scatter about the trend by more than their sample sizes explain (pulled toward the average for a pollster with few polls), times the pollster's rating squared (its grade out of 3: an A+ pollster keeps its full weight, a B 0.54, a C+ 0.36, an unrated pollster 0.25), halved for partisan or internal polls, reduced when a tracking poll's field overlaps its previous release, split between versions of the same survey, and fading with age (its precision halves every 60 days). Influence on today is how much the poll moves today's trend value: recent, large polls carry the estimate, polls from months ago contribute almost nothing, and a pollster's older polls count slightly against its newest because they set its house effect. No pollster's polls may carry more than 15 % of today's value: a pollster over that share has all its weights scaled down until it sits at 15 % (marked †). Sources. Race polls come from the polling tables of Wikipedia's 2026 Senate, governor and House race pages, the VoteHub polling API, the pollresults.org race pages, the Polling USA and Political Poll Bot posts on Bluesky and pollster releases entered by hand; a poll carried by two sources is kept once. National polls come from the same sources except Wikipedia.
