AIFS single vs AIFS-ENS control — a live deterministic bake-off

The hypothesis: ECMWF's deterministic AIFS is trained on an MSE-type objective, which rewards hedging — it blurs with lead time toward an ensemble-mean-like state. The AIFS-ENS members are trained on a CRPS-type objective that punishes blurring, so the control keeps a realistic spectrum ten days out. If the control matches the single on headline scores when both are compared at the same scale, the control — published minutes later — is simply the better deterministic product. This page tests that on every 00Z and 12Z run: both models are truncated to the same spherical-harmonic degree before scoring, the truth is independent radiosondes from the sounding explorer (with ERA5 alongside), and the power spectra that motivate the whole question are shown by lead.

Method

Same spectrum, not just same grid. Each 0.25° global field is expanded in spherical harmonics (pyshtools). Its power spectrum is kept, and both models are truncated at the same degree (T120, about 1.5°) before anything is scored — the blurred model is not punished at scales the sharp one carries, and the sharp model is not punished for realistic small-scale displacement. A 1.5° block mean, which this page used until September 2026, still passed much of the 300–600 km power; the old method is kept as a selectable ERA5 series so the change is visible.

Independent truth. ERA5 is an IFS analysis with its own smoothness, and both models were trained on it. The primary truth here is the radiosonde network the sounding explorer mirrors every hour: 500 hPa height is interpolated in log-pressure from each station's own sounding, and the sounding's surface temperature stands in for 2 m temperature at the same sites and times. Stations are sampled from the truncated fields by bilinear interpolation. ERA5 scores (NH 20–80°N, cosine-weighted, 1.5°) are kept alongside, from the same truncated fields, and are the only truth for MSLP. Radiosonde scores arrive within hours of the valid time; ERA5's lag ~6 days.

Scores. RMSE, bias, anomaly correlation and activity ratio, leads 24–240 h, per run; the page shows the mean across runs with a ±1 standard-error band. Anomalies use the ERA5 1991–2020 ±7-day day-of-year climatology (WeatherBench2), interpolated to the stations; it covers the Northern Hemisphere, so anomaly scores are NH-only while RMSE and bias are also reported globally.

The stakes. If the control matches the single on RMSE/ACC at the common scale while keeping an activity ratio near 1, the deterministic AIFS is dominated: use the control, a few minutes later on the feed, and get realistic sharpness for free — plus 50 siblings quantifying its uncertainty.
