AIFS single versus AIFS-ENS member 0, spectrally matched

The question: as deterministic forecasts, how does ECMWF's AIFS single compare with member 0 of AIFS-ENS? The two are trained differently. The single is trained to minimise squared error, which rewards hedging: it blurs what it is unsure about as lead time grows. The ensemble members are trained on a CRPS-type objective that keeps each run sharp. RMSE and anomaly correlation reward smoothing, so a raw comparison would partly measure blur rather than forecast quality. Here member 0 is first passed through a spectral filter that gives it the single's power spectrum at each lead, then both are truncated at T120 and scored against independent radiosondes from the sounding explorer, with ERA5 alongside. Member 0 as published and the ensemble mean are shown as references; the ensemble mean averages 26 runs, so it is not a single model run.

Method

Why the matching. The AIFS single minimises squared error, so as its forecast loses predictability it damps the features it cannot place; that lowers RMSE whether or not the forecast is better. Member 0 of AIFS-ENS is trained to stay sharp. To compare them as deterministic forecasts on equal terms, member 0's spherical-harmonic coefficients are multiplied, degree by degree, by f(l) = min(1, √(Psingle(l) / Pmember 0(l))), where P are the two models' mean power spectra at the same lead over the previous 40 runs (a 5-degree running mean of the ratio; the global mean, l = 0, is left alone). The filter uses only past forecasts, never the verifying data, so it is a transformation the forecaster could apply in real time. It only removes power (f ≤ 1). The spectra panel shows how closely the filtered member 0 reproduces the single. The filter matches the average spectrum; it cannot copy the single's flow-dependent damping (smoothing more where the flow is less predictable), so part of what remains may still be hedging done well rather than a better-placed forecast.

Same scale. Every 0.25° global field is expanded in spherical harmonics (pyshtools) and all series are truncated at T120 (about 1.5°) before scoring, so no model is judged at scales it does not share with the others. A 1.5° block mean, which this page used until September 2026, still passed much of the 300–600 km power.

Independent truth. ERA5 is an IFS analysis that both models were trained on. The primary truth is the radiosonde network the sounding explorer mirrors every hour: 500 hPa height and 850 hPa temperature are interpolated in log-pressure from each station's sounding, and the forecasts are sampled at the stations by bilinear interpolation. ERA5 scores (NH 20–80°N, cosine-weighted, 1.5°) come from the same truncated fields and are the only truth for MSLP and 2 m temperature. Radiosonde scores arrive within hours; ERA5 lags about 6 days.

Scores and tests. RMSE, bias, anomaly correlation and activity ratio at leads 24–240 h, per run. Station anomalies use each station's own day-of-year median from its full IGRA record; ERA5 anomalies use the ERA5 1991–2020 ±7-day climatology (WeatherBench2), including 850 hPa temperature since September 2026. Differences from the single are tested with a paired moving-block bootstrap: runs are paired by start time and resampled in blocks of four consecutive runs (two days) to allow for serial correlation, 2,000 times; the 95 % percentile interval is shown and a difference is called significant only when it excludes zero.

The ensemble mean. The mean of the control and 25 perturbed AIFS-ENS members is shown for reference. Averaging removes the unpredictable part of the forecast, so it usually wins on RMSE at longer leads while having much less variance than the atmosphere; it is not a single model run and is not the page's question.
