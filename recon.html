What is shown, and how the data are checked
  • Track: the high-density observations (HDOB), one every 30 s: position, flight-level pressure and height, the extrapolated surface pressure (only below the 550 hPa level, as in the format; above it the message carries a D-value instead), temperature, dewpoint, the 30-s flight-level wind and the peak 10-s wind, the SFMR surface wind and rain rate.
  • Quality flags: every HDOB line ends with two flags set by the aircraft. A flagged field is removed, not drawn: position (first digit 1 or 3), pressure and height (2 or 3), temperature and dewpoint (second digit 1, 4, 5 or 9), flight-level wind (2, 4, 6, 9) and SFMR (3, 5, 6, 9). Missing values (///) are gaps. On top of that every field has a physical range check, a dewpoint above the temperature is dropped, and a position that jumps more than 650 kt from both neighbours is dropped. Track segments with no valid value of the chosen variable are drawn thin and grey.
  • Mission maxima are taken within 300 n mi of the storm centre (interpolated best track), so the ferry legs and the departure airfield do not count.
  • Dropsondes (TEMP DROP): the mandatory levels, the significant temperature levels and the 21212 wind levels merged into one profile; heights are integrated from the splash level (the sea surface) and tied to the coded mandatory-level heights. Temperature at a wind-only level is interpolated in log pressure between the neighbouring significant levels (that is what significant levels guarantee). The surface wind estimates follow Franklin et al. (2003): 0.83 times the mean wind in the lowest 150 m (WL150) and 0.80 times the mean of the lowest 500 m (MBL). The skew-T is the sounding explorer itself, with the same analysis.
  • Vortex fixes (vortex data messages): the centre position and minimum pressure at the fix time, with the inbound and outbound maximum flight-level winds placed at their bearing and range from the centre.
  • Storm: a mission belongs to the storm in its mission number (e.g. 0209A = storm 09); a surveillance mission without one (the NOAA G-IV) is put with the nearest storm and says so. Best track and official forecast: NHC ATCF b-deck and forecast file.
  • Tail Doppler radar (NOAA WP-3D and G-IV only): the real-time graphics of NOAA AOML's Hurricane Research Division, mirrored about every 15 minutes. They appear about 70 minutes after each centre fix (analysis about 50 minutes, transmission, graphics). Each swath is one analysis from one pass through the storm (2 km by 2 km by 0.5 km grid); the composites, azimuthal means, tilt and zoom merge the passes so far around the centre found by the Fischer et al. (2022, 2024) method, or, when none is found, around the centre the radar operator gave. HRD warns that the composites may not be storm centred and may not be meaningful in storms weaker than hurricanes: check the individual swaths first. The high-resolution in/outbound profiles are no longer made in real time from 2026. TDR centres (0.5, 2, 3 and 6 km) come from HRD's centre files; a file that names a different analysis, or whose centre is more than 100 km from the vortex-message fixes (200 km from the best track when there is no fix), is not drawn. A flight is matched to its NHC mission by aircraft (H = NOAA2 N42RF, I = NOAA3 N43RF, N = NOAA9 N49RF), storm name and time. Kept: the swaths at 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 10 km for every analysis and every composite; the azimuthal means, tilt, zoom and centre map for the latest composite only.
  • Centre track and passes: the centre at any moment is interpolated in time between the vortex fixes of all missions (and the flight's TDR centres, when there are any); beyond the fixes the nearest fix is moved with the best-track motion. A centre pass is the closest approach within 20 n mi; each pass gives an inbound and an outbound leg, cut at 150 n mi or where the aircraft turns back. The radial profiles, the per-leg maxima in the intensity history and the storm-relative plot all use this track.
  • Satellite: GOES-East ABI band 13 (10.3 µm) brightness temperature at each centre pass, from NOAA's open data on AWS: a mesoscale sector when one covers the storm (1-minute images), otherwise CONUS or the full disk; the image nearest the pass within 10 minutes, resampled to the map's projection. Pixels the ABI flags as out of range or missing are left empty. No passive-microwave imagery: the freely redistributable source (NOAA's TC PRIMED) is not updated in real time (it held no 2026 Atlantic storm in October 2026), and the real-time sources need a login or do not permit redistribution.
  • Alerts (opt in): the browser's notifications, from this page only while it is open; nothing is sent from a server.
  • Freshness: a mirror in GitHub Actions reads the NHC archive every 15 minutes, and again 7 minutes later while a mission is airborne; NHC posts each message a few minutes after it is sent. The page asks for new data every 90 s while a mission is flying.

Data: NOAA National Hurricane Center reconnaissance archive (USAF 53rd Weather Reconnaissance Squadron and NOAA Aircraft Operations Center), nhc.noaa.gov; best track and forecast from NHC's ATCF files. Tail Doppler radar: NOAA AOML Hurricane Research Division. Satellite: NOAA GOES-East (GOES-16 to April 2025, GOES-19 since), NOAA Open Data Dissemination on AWS. Decoded here, not an official product; for operational use see the NHC.
