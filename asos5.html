US airport five-minute observations

Loading live observations…

Tap a card to isolate a station (Esc or tap again to clear) · drag across the chart to zoom, double-click to reset · times are each station’s local clock

Sources. Live: Iowa State’s IEM observation history, fetched in your browser every 2 minutes — every 5-minute ASOS row the station transmitted plus every METAR and SPECI. The 5-minute rows carry temperature and dewpoint in whole degrees Celsius (hence the 1.8 °F steps in the °F traces); the hourly METARs and SPECIs carry tenths, and the cards use them whenever one is as recent as the latest 5-minute row. The NWS API is the fallback when IEM cannot serve a station, and the NOAA/NWS MADIS 5-minute feed is layered in where the background mirror has it. The 5-minute feed reaches the public networks about 10–15 minutes after the observation; nothing public is faster. Yesterday’s data are kept in your browser, so a return visit only fetches today.

Max/min. Today’s are the extremes since local midnight: the samples merged with the official METAR 6-hour groups (1sTTT/2sTTT) where those are more extreme. Yesterday’s row is the official 24-hour climate day (4snTxTxTxsnTnTnTn).

Wind. Direction is where the wind blows from, in degrees true. Arrows on the cards point the way the air is moving; barbs follow the usual convention (pennant 50 kt, full barb 10, half barb 5, always in knots). Veering = turning clockwise (e.g. south to west), backing = counter-clockwise; the change is measured between the 30-minute vector mean now and 1 and 3 hours earlier, and only when the wind is at least 5 kt at both times. “Peak wind” is the official METAR remark (PK WND dddff/hhmm, the highest instantaneous wind since the previous hourly report, at least 26 kt); “wind shift” is the WSHFT remark (a change of 45° or more in under 15 minutes with sustained wind of 10 kt or more; FROPA = a front passed). dddVddd variable-direction ranges are drawn as bars on the direction panel.
