SFS subseasonal outlook

NOAA's Seasonal Forecast System — the coupled UFS successor to CFSv2, still experimental (beta2) — runs 31 members from the first of each month. Anomalies are against its own 1991–2020 reforecast for the same start month and lead, so the model's drift is not read as a forecast. Weekly maps and terciles for weeks 1–6 and the daily animation, in the same layout as the GEFS and GEPS pages, and how its hindcasts score against those two. An issue appears about five days after the 1st, so by mid-month its first one or two weeks are already past. Its monthly and seasonal forecast is on the seasonal page.

Checking NOAA's daily forecast store for this issue…

    Every issue is checked before anything is drawn: all forecast days filled, the 35–65°N temperature trend across the forecast close to the reforecast's for the same start month, and the daily fields averaged over the first month matching the issue's own monthly mean. The weekly maps, terciles and animation appear here by themselves the first month NOAA's daily store passes. The monthly forecast, which is sound, is on the seasonal page; the hindcast comparison (Verification, in the menu) uses the reforecast, which is sound too.

    How it is built

    On the first of each month NOAA's SFS beta2 runs 31 members to day 46; NOAA publishes them about five days later. Each field is regridded to the GEFS page's 1.5° grid and every forecast day is differenced from the mean of SFS's own reforecast at the same lead — 11 members from the same start date of every year 1991–2020, which removes the model's drift and its mean bias together; because the reforecast spans the normal period itself, no further re-basing is needed. 500 hPa height has its global mean removed so the circulation shows. The tercile maps count the 31 members against the terciles of the reforecast's own weekly anomalies (330 per gridpoint and week), so their normal is 1991–2020, not the 2016–2025 terciles of the GEFS and GEPS pages: part of any “above normal” here is the warming since then. The skill masks hatch wherever the reforecast ensemble mean's anomaly correlation with ERA5, over the starts of the same month with a leave-one-year-out climatology, is not significantly above zero (one-sided 5 % test, false-discovery rate 10 % across the map): temperature worldwide on the 1991–2025 starts, precipitation over the Northern Hemisphere on 1991–2023, the extent of the site's ERA5 precipitation store. NOAA's own published climatology files are not used: the October one was found to be a copy of September's. The hindcast comparison scores SFS, GEFSv12 and GEPS on 38 starts (1 September and 1 October, 2001–2019; for GEFSv12 the Wednesday start within 3 days of the 1st) as four-member means against ERA5.

    Where the data come from

    The model. NOAA's Seasonal Forecast System (SFS) is a fully coupled Earth-system model built on the Unified Forecast System (UFS) and the planned successor to the Climate Forecast System, version 2 (CFSv2). NOAA describes the SFS beta as an FV3 atmosphere (hydrostatic) with Common Community Physics Package physics largely from GFSv17, the MOM6 ocean model, the CICE6 sea-ice model and the Noah-MP land model. It is developed by NOAA's National Weather Service (NCEP Environmental Modeling Center) and Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research (Physical Sciences Laboratory) together with many developers across NOAA and the wider community — application-team co-leads Neil Barton (EMC), Phil Pegion (PSL) and Xiaqiong (Kate) Zhou (EMC) — with funding from the NWS Office of Science and Technology Integration Modeling Program and the OAR Weather Program Office's Subseasonal-to-Seasonal Program. The beta has run in near-real time since March 2026 as experimental guidance for NOAA's Climate Prediction Center. This page uses beta2, NOAA's second experimental configuration: it is a research prototype, not an operational NOAA forecast.

    The files. NOAA publishes the forecasts and reforecasts through its Open Data Dissemination (NODD) program on Amazon Web Services, in the bucket noaa-oar-sfsdev-pds, folder experiments/beta2/: forecast/YYYYMM/ holds each issue (atmosphere and ocean, daily and monthly Zarr stores, 31 members from the first of the month) and reforecast/MM/ the reforecasts (11 members, every year 1991–2025; so far for September and October starts only). This site reads those Zarr stores anonymously over HTTPS in GitHub Actions, keeps only the fields and regions it draws, and builds its own reforecast climatology from them rather than using NOAA's climatology files.

    Terms. NOAA data distributed through NODD are free and open to the public; credit NOAA when reusing them. Everything done to them here — regridding, anomalies, terciles, skill masks, the daily-store check and the recovery of interleaved issues — is this site's own processing, not NOAA's, and NOAA has not reviewed it. More about the system: NOAA EPIC: the SFS beta near-real-time launch (2026).

    The other data on this page. ERA5 reanalysis (Copernicus Climate Change Service, ECMWF), the truth for the skill masks and the hindcast scores; the GEFSv12 reforecast (NOAA, NODD); the GEPS hindcast (Data Source: Environment and Climate Change Canada, under the ECCC Data Servers End-use Licence); and CFSv2 (NOAA NCEP) from the IRI Data Library's SubX and NMME archives and NOAA's operational CFSv2 files on AWS, for the comparison with the system SFS is meant to replace.
