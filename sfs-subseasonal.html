SFS subseasonal outlook

NOAA's Seasonal Forecast System — the coupled UFS successor to CFSv2, still experimental (beta2) — runs 31 members from the first of each month. Anomalies are against its own 1991–2020 reforecast for the same start month and lead, so the model's drift is not read as a forecast. Weekly maps and terciles for weeks 1–6 and the daily animation, in the same layout as the GEFS and GEPS pages, and how its hindcasts score against those two. Its monthly and seasonal forecast is on the seasonal page.

Checking NOAA's daily forecast store for this issue…

    Every issue is checked before anything is drawn: all forecast days filled, the 35–65°N temperature trend across the forecast close to the reforecast's for the same start month, and the daily fields averaged over the first month matching the issue's own monthly mean. The weekly maps, terciles and animation appear here by themselves the first month NOAA's daily store passes. The monthly forecast, which is sound, is on the seasonal page; the hindcast comparison (Verification, in the menu) uses the reforecast, which is sound too.

    How it is built

    On the first of each month NOAA's SFS beta2 runs 31 members to day 46; NOAA publishes them about five days later. Each field is regridded to the GEFS page's 1.5° grid and every forecast day is differenced from the mean of SFS's own reforecast at the same lead — 11 members from the same start date of every year 1991–2020, which removes the model's drift and its mean bias together; because the reforecast spans the normal period itself, no further re-basing is needed. 500 hPa height has its global mean removed so the circulation shows. The tercile maps count the 31 members against the terciles of the reforecast's own weekly anomalies (330 per gridpoint and week). The skill masks hatch wherever the reforecast ensemble mean's anomaly correlation with ERA5, over the 1991–2025 starts of the same month with a leave-one-year-out climatology, is below 0.25 (temperature worldwide; precipitation over the Northern Hemisphere only, where the local ERA5 record has it). NOAA's own published climatology files are not used: the October one was found to be a copy of September's. The hindcast comparison scores SFS, GEFSv12 and GEPS on the same 38 start dates (1 September and 1 October, 2001–2019) as four-member means against ERA5.
