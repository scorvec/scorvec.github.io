Method

Candidate centers are local sea-level-pressure minima on each member's own 0.25° field that are at least 2 hPa deeper than the mean pressure on a surrounding ~5° ring (a closed-circulation test), over water, equatorward of 30° at genesis (20° in the winter hemisphere, where subtropical lows masquerade as TCs), and outside climatologically cyclone-free seas. Centers are linked across daily steps with a motion-extrapolated 800 km gate, kept when the track lives ≥3 days with peak 10 m winds ≥14 m/s, truncated at 42° latitude as an extratropical-transition proxy, required to spend at least 40% of their fixes over water (landfalling storms pass; lows living on or along land do not), and required to travel at least 700 km along-track — quasi-stationary monsoon and heat lows fail that test, which together with a Gulf-of-Aden exclusion keeps the July Somali-jet trough from being re-labelled as a new storm every few days. Tracks from all members are clustered into storms when genesis points agree within 600 km and two days; only storms supported by ≥15% of members are shown, so what survives is the coherent ensemble signal rather than single-member noise. Monsoon depressions (Bay of Bengal, Arabian Sea) legitimately pass these gates and are shown — they are TC-family systems.

No bogus/relocation data is used: tracks come purely from the forecast fields, so position and existence uncertainty are the ensemble's own. Support percentages are genesis probabilities in the loose sense (share of members producing a matching storm), and the strike-probability shading counts members whose track passes within 200 km of a point at any time in the 15 days.
