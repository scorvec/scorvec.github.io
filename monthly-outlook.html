Statistical forecast and GEFS

The statistical forecast, the 35-day GEFS run of the 15th (31 members): its forecast of the target month's days it reaches and, for temperature, its “lead-in” (the days before the month), and the blend. The blend's weights come from the 2000–2019 GEFS reforecast, at each point and smoothed over about 2500 km, fitted against the whole month, so they also say how much of each part carries into the monthly mean; the blend is issued only where it beat the statistical forecast in that cross-validated hindcast. The scores and weights above each map are on those same years, all against the whole month.

How the statistical forecast is built

Each component's forecast multiplied by its consolidation weight at each point, and their sum (the outlook, before the skill mask). The weights come from a non-negative regression of the observed anomalies on the five components' cross-validated hindcasts, smoothed over about 2500 km; a component that never helped in a region gets no weight there.

Record

Every issued forecast, scored once its month is over against ERA5 over 20–90N: the anomaly correlation (ACC) of the forecast; the same for the trend component alone (what “it will be warmer than 1991–2020” scores by itself) and for the C3S multi-system mean, the seven dynamical seasonal models issued two days earlier, at the same target month (2 m temperature and precipitation; C3S publishes no 500 hPa height anomalies); and the RPSS of the tercile probabilities. The benchmarks are yardsticks only: nothing from them enters the forecast.

How it is built

What it is. The coming calendar month's mean 2 m temperature, precipitation and 500 hPa height over the Northern Hemisphere, as expected anomalies against the 1991–2020 normal and as tercile probabilities. The method was designed and built by Claude (Anthropic's AI model) and runs unattended on the 15th of every month. Its core is a statistical forecast that reads no dynamical model: five empirical components — the trend, a RONI, QBO and polar-vortex regression, a constructed analogue on the tropical oceans, local persistence of temperature, sea ice, snow and soil moisture, and the closest analogue years — are blended with weights learned from a cross-validated hindcast back to 1981. That statistical forecast is then blended with the whole 35-day GEFS run of the 15th, which reaches the 18th or 19th of the target month, wherever the 2000–2019 GEFS reforecast shows the blend beats it, and each forecast is scored against ERA5 and against the C3S multi-system seasonal forecast once its month is over. More from the models on the seasonal page and the GEPS and GEFS outlooks.

Reading the maps. Expected anomaly: the forecast monthly-mean anomaly; on the height map the contours are the total monthly-mean 500 hPa height (normal plus anomaly, every 6 dam). Its amplitudes are those of an expected value, always smaller than any single month's anomaly. Tercile probabilities: the chance that the month falls in the upper or lower third of its 1991–2020 record at each point, from that point's own climatology shifted by the forecast and narrowed by the hindcast's error; shaded where one of the two is the most likely category and reaches 40 % (CPC's convention), and equal chances where the hindcast has no significant skill. With the skill mask on, the map is faded and hatched where the hindcast of this calendar month has no significant skill (one-sided test, false-discovery rate 10 % across the map): read those areas as “no better than the normal”; with it off, the probabilities are shown raw. Click a map to open it full size.

Timing. The outlook for month M is issued on the 15th of month M−1. Its predictors are ERA5 means over the ~30 days that end on the 7th of M−1 (from the 8th of M−2): ERA5's preliminary release (ERA5T) trails real time by about six days, so on the 15th that window is always complete. The lead from the end of the predictors to the middle of the target month is about five and a half weeks. The hindcast reproduces exactly this timing for every year.

Data. ERA5 500 hPa height, 2 m temperature, precipitation, SST, sea ice, snow depth, topsoil moisture and 50 hPa wind at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC (precipitation as 6-hour totals, and from 2023 as hourly totals every 3 hours, which match the full totals to a correlation of 0.997), 1959 onward: the WeatherBench 2 1.5° copy to 2022 and the ARCO-ERA5 0.25° store from 2023 (ERA5, then ERA5T), both box-averaged onto 3° cells and kept as half-month means (days 1–7 and 8 to the month's end). The two sources were compared over 2022, which both hold: the hemispheric differences are 0.6 m in 500 hPa height and 0.3 K in 2 m temperature (larger along steep coasts and mountains, from the different regridding), and that 2022 difference is removed from the ARCO years month by month.

The five components. Trend: at each point, the linear trend of the anomaly of the three months centred on the target month over the 30 years before the target year, extrapolated to that year. Only past years enter, so it is a forecast the same way in the hindcast as in real time, and the part of each year's anomaly explained by relative Niño-3.4 is removed before the line is fitted: a run of La Niña winters at the end of the window would otherwise be extrapolated as climate change. RONI, QBO and vortex regression: the detrended target-month anomaly regressed on four indices of the predictor window: relative Niño-3.4 (the Niño-3.4 SST anomaly minus the 20S–20N mean, the quantity behind CPC's RONI; van Oldenborgh et al. 2021) split into its El Niño and La Niña halves, because the two teleconnections are not mirror images; the equatorial zonal-mean wind at 50 hPa (the QBO); and the 57–63N zonal-mean wind at 50 hPa (the lower polar vortex). Tropical constructed analogue (Van den Dool 1994): the window's detrended SST and rainfall anomalies between 20S and 20N (each scaled to the same total variance) are written as a ridge-regularised combination of every library year's, and the same weights combine those years' detrended target-month anomalies. It carries ENSO's flavour, the Indian Ocean and the tropical Atlantic together, and the rainfall is the heating that actually drives the teleconnections: adding it raised the 500 hPa hindcast correlation in all four months tested (on average from 0.13 to 0.15) and precipitation's from 0.13 to 0.15. Only the tropics are used: there the ocean drives the atmosphere through convection, while poleward of about 20° most of an SST anomaly is the footprint of the circulation that made it (the PDO follows the Aleutian low, the Atlantic tripole the NAO), so it would mostly re-encode atmospheric persistence. Local persistence: at each point, the detrended target anomaly regressed on the window's anomaly of the same field; for 2 m temperature also on the window's sea-ice concentration, snow depth and topsoil moisture, the surface memory that lets a warm Arctic sea, an early snowpack or a dry spring carry into the next month, and for precipitation on topsoil moisture (a surface predictor with negligible anomalies at a point is left out there). Analogue years: the eight library years whose window state is closest — relative Niño-3.4, the QBO, the vortex, the leading tropical SST patterns and the leading hemispheric 500 hPa patterns — and the mean of their detrended target-month anomalies.

A larger library. The regression and persistence components are trained on the target month pooled with its two neighbours (each with its own predictor window and anomalies), so every coefficient rests on about 200 cases instead of about 65; the constructed analogue and the analogue years use the target month alone, which scored better for them.

Detrending. “Detrended” means the anomaly minus a tricube local-linear fit (20-year half-width) of the 3-month anomaly through the record. That fit sees the target year's own value and its neighbours, the one place where the hindcast is not strictly out of sample; its effect is on the slow part only, which the trend component (strictly past-only) carries.

Consolidation. At every point, a non-negative least-squares regression of the observed anomaly on the five components' hindcasts, with the normal equations smoothed by a 2500 km Gaussian and damped toward zero, so the weights vary only on large scales and shrink the outlook where the components did not verify. Because the components and the weights are fitted on the hindcast, the skill shown is cross-validated twice over: each hindcast year's components leave out that year and its neighbours, and its consolidation weights leave out that year.

GEFS guidance. GEFS runs to 35 days from 00Z every day, so the run of the 15th reaches the 18th or 19th of the target month (the 21st of March). The whole run is used, as two predictors: its ensemble-mean forecast of the target month's days it reaches, and of its days before the month (the lead-in, the state the month starts from; temperature only, below). Both are the GEFS page's own anomalies (against the model's 2000–2019 reforecast climatology at the same start date and lead, which removes its drift and bias, re-based to 1991–2020 with ERA5; 500 hPa height with its global mean removed), its weekly means weighted by how many of each window's days they hold. The hindcast is the 2000–2019 GEFSv12 reforecast (four members, the Wednesday start nearest the 15th of each year, so the target window is 15 to 21 days long, 18 on average; anomalies against the other years), reduced the same way. At each point the blend is a non-negative regression of the observed monthly anomaly on the statistical forecast and the run's two parts, fitted on those 20 years and smoothed over about 2500 km, and cross-validated the same way as everything else (each year's weights without that year). Stopping the target window at day 15 scored the same. The lead-in earned weight only for temperature (about 0.09), where the persistence of what the run lays down before the month helps a little; for height and precipitation it earned little (0.06, 0.02) and made the blend slightly worse, so it is left out of those. The statistical forecast's weight is pulled toward 1, since it is already calibrated on its own 1981-onward hindcast: refitted freely on 20 years it fell to about 0.3, a sampling accident of those years that would have damped the whole forecast, and the anchored fit scored as well or better. Over the twelve calendar months the blend raised the hindcast's temperature anomaly correlation on those years from 0.20 to 0.30 (RPSS 0.02 to 0.06), the 500 hPa height's from 0.08 to 0.14 and precipitation's from 0.12 to 0.15 (its mean-square error fell by under 1 %). It is issued for a variable only when it beats the statistical forecast on those years; its probabilities then use the statistical forecast's error spread scaled by the blend's error ratio, and its skill mask adds the points where the blend's own 20-year hindcast is significant. The live run has 31 members, the reforecast four, so the live GEFS signal is cleaner than the one the weights were fitted on and the blend leans toward the statistical forecast a little more than it needs to. If no GEFS run of the 12th–15th is available, the statistical forecast is issued alone.

Probabilities. Each point's tercile boundaries are the 33rd and 67th percentiles of its 1991–2020 monthly anomalies. The forecast distribution is that point's own 1991–2020 climatology, shifted by the forecast anomaly and narrowed by the hindcast's error relative to the climatological spread, so a skill-less forecast of zero is exactly climatology (a third each) and a skewed variable such as precipitation keeps its skew. Where the hindcast has no significant skill the probabilities are set to equal chances, as CPC does; without that, precipitation's probabilities scored below climatology (RPSS −0.03), and with it they do no harm (about 0.00) while temperature keeps nearly all of its +0.06 to +0.16. The hindcast RPSS is computed the same way, each year's equal-chances mask and error spread fitted without that year. With the skill mask off, the probability maps show the raw probabilities.

Skill beyond the trend. Much of any monthly forecast's correlation now comes from warming alone, so the skill figures also score the forecast against the trend component used by itself: a mean-square skill score above zero is what the other four components add.

Benchmark. Once its month is over, each forecast is scored beside the C3S multi-system seasonal forecast issued in the same month (the seven systems' ensemble means at lead month 2, which is the same target month, from the tables the seasonal page keeps), moved to the 1991–2020 base with ERA5. C3S publishes no 500 hPa height anomalies, so it is compared on temperature and precipitation. Nothing from it enters the forecast.

Tested and left out. Every candidate was run through the same cross-validated hindcast and kept only if it helped. The MJO: an RMM index built from ERA5 1979–2022 on the site's own Wheeler–Hendon EOFs (correlation 0.97 with the reference RMM). Its state in the week before issue lowered the skill slightly, and even a perfect 15-day MJO forecast (the observed index over days 15–29) added only about +0.01 to the 500 hPa correlation in December to February and nothing to temperature: its teleconnections reach only the first week or two of the target month and wash out of the monthly mean, and the slower part of the tropical heating is already in the constructed analogue's rainfall. The 10 hPa vortex: the 60N wind from MERRA-2 (1980 onward), added to or in place of the 50 hPa index, changed the December to March skill by ±0.007; a stratospheric warming that matters mostly happens after the issue date. A warming-index trend (linear in the trailing 3-year hemispheric temperature instead of in time): better in January, worse in November and July, no better overall. 90-day windows for SST, soil moisture and sea ice: no better, and worse for April. Extratropical SST in the analogue (left out for the reason above), weighting the blend toward ENSO-like years (slightly worse, strong El Niño years included), and dressing the probabilities with the hindcast's own errors (worse than either way above).

What it is not. No seasonal model enters (C3S is only the yardstick), and GEFS reaches only the 18th or 19th of the target month: beyond that the forecast is the statistics' alone. Anomalies are against 1991–2020; skill is reported for the calendar month being forecast, so it changes from issue to issue (summer temperature scores best, late-autumn 500 hPa height worst).
