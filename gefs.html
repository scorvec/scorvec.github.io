How it is built

Each day's 00Z GEFS run is read member by member from NOAA's open-data bucket once it reaches day 35. A forecast day is the mean of the 12Z and following 00Z fields (precipitation summed and outgoing longwave averaged over the four six-hour windows), so day 1 is the start date itself; fields are box-averaged to 1.5°. Exactly the same arithmetic is applied to the GEFSv12 reforecast — the 35-day Wednesday runs of 2000–2019, four members each, pooled within ±15 days of the start date in any year, counted on a 365-day calendar so that leap years line up — and every day of the forecast is differenced from the reforecast mean at the same lead, which removes the model's drift and its mean bias together. Today's GEFS does not drift quite as its reforecast did — checked against its own analyses it runs cooler at long lead in the northern mid-latitudes and in the stratosphere — so the difference, measured daily from the 45 most recent runs whose 35 days have been observed (zonal mean, by lead and latitude), is removed as well (each run's archived ensemble mean, its control where none was archived; those runs started 36 to 80 days earlier, so the estimate reflects the season of one to two months before); the Drift correction figure shows it. The reference is then moved to 1991–2020 by the observed ERA5 shift between the periods (ERA5 2001–2020 standing in for 2000–2019), with no model in that step. 500 hPa height has its global mean removed so the circulation shows. Because GEFS's reforecast reaches 10 hPa, the polar vortex is corrected the same way at every lead, against ERA5. Tercile probabilities are calibrated on the same reforecasts, each start verified against a climatology and an ERA5 normal that both leave its own season out, and the MJO is scored the same way; the teleconnection calibration is fitted on every reforecast start within ±45 days, each de-drifted against a climatology and re-based with an ERA5 normal that leave its own season out, so its skill is out of sample (left in, the shared normal had lifted the week-3 to week-5 correlations by 0.04–0.05). The skill masks hatch wherever the reforecast's anomaly correlation is not significantly above zero (one-sided 5 % test on the 20 reforecast years, false-discovery rate 10 % across the map). The GEPS page's method gives the rest in full; where the two differ (reforecast years and members, start days, the day convention, the season left out, the drift correction), this note describes GEFS.
