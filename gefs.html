How it is built

Each day's 00Z GEFS run is read member by member from NOAA's open-data bucket once it reaches day 35. A forecast day is the mean of the 12Z and following 00Z fields (precipitation summed and outgoing longwave averaged over the four six-hour windows), so day 1 is the start date itself; fields are box-averaged to 1.5°. Exactly the same arithmetic is applied to the GEFSv12 reforecast — the 35-day Wednesday runs of 2000–2019, four members each, pooled within ±15 days of the start date — and every day of the forecast is differenced from the reforecast mean at the same lead, which removes the model's drift and its mean bias together. Today's GEFS does not drift quite as its reforecast did — checked against its own analyses it runs cooler at long lead in the northern mid-latitudes and in the stratosphere — so the difference, measured daily from the 45 most recent runs whose 35 days have been observed (zonal mean, by lead and latitude), is removed as well; the Drift correction figure shows it. The reference is then moved to 1991–2020 by the observed ERA5 shift between the periods (ERA5 2001–2020 standing in for 2000–2019), with no model in that step. 500 hPa height has its global mean removed so the circulation shows. Because GEFS's reforecast reaches 10 hPa, the polar vortex is corrected the same way at every lead, against ERA5. Tercile probabilities, the teleconnection probabilities and the MJO score are calibrated on the same reforecasts, with each start verified against a climatology that leaves its own season out, and the skill masks hatch wherever the reforecast's anomaly correlation falls below 0.25. The GEPS page's full method applies throughout.
