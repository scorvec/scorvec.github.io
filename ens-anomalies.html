The super-ensemble pools the IFS-ENS, AIFS-ENS, GEFS and GEPS runs from the same start, with equal weight per ensemble rather than per member: a quarter each, so the 51 members of AIFS-ENS count as much as the 21 of GEPS (by member, the two ECMWF ensembles would carry two thirds of the vote). It is computed on the 0.5° cells from each ensemble's members averaged over the cells, on the days all four runs hold (15 from 00 UTC, 14 from 12 UTC), and only where every ensemble has enough complete members, as on its own maps. Its anomaly is the mean of the four ensemble-mean anomalies, each against its own normal; the dots mark where the weighted share of members agreeing on the sign reaches the gate of a two-sided sign test at 5 % on the weighted count (exact, about 59 % with all 153 members, below any single ensemble's because more members are behind it). Its change is from the super-ensemble run 12, 24, 36 or 48 h earlier, Welch's test on the weighted means (the variance of each ensemble's mean, weighted, with Satterthwaite's degrees of freedom), dotted at the same 10 % false discovery rate. It runs from 00 and 12 UTC only, because GEPS runs no 06 or 18 UTC forecast and IFS-ENS's reach day 5. A run is made again when one of its four is (IFS-ENS's 2 m temperature from the 0.1° files, hours after the rest). The clusters view groups the same four ensembles' members, also a quarter each, and the regimes view shows each ensemble beside their equal-weight mean.

A run is shown as it arrives: its days 1–5 as soon as they are complete on the model’s server, days 6–10 next, and the rest, with the change from earlier runs, the regimes and the clusters, once the whole run is in (the run picker and the caption say how far a run has got).

Each ensemble is shown on its own published grid, untouched: the finer grids are not smoothed and the coarser ones are not interpolated before the colours (your browser interpolates between grid points as it draws). The page keeps each ensemble's four newest runs, and the buttons beside “Run” pick one (the newest is shown until another is picked; the regimes' timeline and its comparisons take the runs picked). The ensembles' runs can be hours apart, and the date line under the buttons always gives the calendar days a map covers. The clusters always pool each ensemble's newest run that reaches day 15.

Days and periods

A day is a calendar day in UTC, the mean of the 06, 12, 18 and 24 UTC fields. A 00 UTC run has 15 full days (day 1 is the day it starts), a 06, 12 or 18 UTC run 14 (day 1 is the next day; its first and last part-days are left out); GEFS and GEPS, which run to 16 days, have one more, and an IFS-ENS run from 06 or 18 UTC, which ECMWF runs to 144 hours, has 5. The periods are days 1–5, 6–10 and 11–15 (11–14 for a 06, 12 or 18 UTC run), each the mean of its days. A member that misses any field of a period's days is left out of that period, and a period from fewer than 80 % of the ensemble's members is not drawn.

Anomaly

Each member's mean less the 1991–2020 normal for the same days, and the ensemble mean of those anomalies, shaded everywhere and dotted where at least k of the n members give the anomaly the same sign as their mean: the smallest number a two-sided sign test against an even split passes at 5 % (33 of 50 or 51, 22 of 31, 16 of 21). Without dots the members are split: read the colour there as a hint, not a forecast; the hover says how many agree.

The normals. 2 m temperature: ERA5-Land over land and ERA5 over the sea (ERA5 interpolated from its sea points only, so a coastal mountain does not chill the water beside it), the monthly means at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC, so the normal samples the daily cycle as the forecast day does, on the 0.1° grid of the IFS, whose orography ERA5-Land shares. On the 0.25° and 0.5° grids the normal is the 0.1° one averaged over each cell and moved by 6.5 °C per kilometre from the IFS's height to the model's own, so a valley or a mountain cell is compared with a normal at its own height. The anomalies are the models' raw output against the reanalysis: they include the warming since 1991–2020 (in the 500 hPa height, a positive anomaly across the tropics) and each model's own biases, which no drift correction removes. 500 hPa height and sea-level pressure: ERA5 at 1.5° (the daily means of the four synoptic hours), interpolated to each grid. Each normal is a smooth curve through the year (the mean and three annual harmonics, fitted so that its month averages match the twelve monthly normals), read at each day. The sea-level pressure anomaly is not drawn where the model's ground is above 1,500 m: there the pressure “at sea level” is an extrapolation that each model and the reanalysis make differently.

Standardized anomalies (σ)

For 500 hPa height and sea-level pressure the anomaly can also be shown in standard deviations: the ensemble-mean anomaly divided by the 1991–2020 standard deviation of an anomaly over the same averaging period — a single day for the daily maps, the period's own 4- or 5-day mean for the periods (a 5-day mean varies less than a day does, so dividing it by a day's spread would understate it). The standard deviations come from ERA5 at 1.5° (the same daily means as the normals): each cell's anomalies from the normal curve, a linear trend over 1991–2020 removed, averaged over the period's length, and their variance through the year fitted as a smooth curve (the mean and three harmonics of a 366-day year), interpolated to each model's grid as the normals are. Colours run to ±3σ; the members-agree gate is the same as for the anomaly. Across the tropics the 500 hPa height reads several standard deviations high: its day-to-day spread is small there, and the warming since 1991–2020 is not. The change maps stay in metres and hectopascals.

Change from an earlier run

This run's ensemble mean less that of the same ensemble's run 12 hours earlier, or of the run picked with the “vs … h earlier” buttons: 6, 12, 18, 24, 36 or 48 hours earlier, whichever the ensemble ran (GEPS runs twice a day, so 12, 24, 36 and 48 only; 48 h is the trend over two days), over the same calendar days, on a common 0.5° grid (each member's field averaged over the 0.5° cells first). The change is shaded everywhere and dotted where it is significant: Welch's t-test on the two runs' members at every point, and the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure over the whole map at a false discovery rate of 10 %, so that among the dotted points at most a tenth are expected to be chance. Shading without dots is a change within what the two runs' members could give by chance: read it as a hint, not an established shift. The last period of a 00 UTC run is compared over days 11–14, the days the earlier run holds, and an IFS-ENS run from 06 or 18 UTC only over the days both runs hold. Where the ensembles are confident (the first days) even a small change can be significant: read the size from the colours.

Spread

How far the members disagree: their standard deviation for each day or period (each member's mean over the period's days), on the same 0.5° cells as the change maps (each member's field averaged over the cells first, so on the finer grids it is the spread of 0.5° cells). vs climate (500 hPa height and sea-level pressure): that spread over the 1991–2020 standard deviation of an anomaly over the same averaging period, from ERA5 as for the standardized anomalies. Near 1 the members are spread as widely as the weather itself varies at that time of year, so the forecast says little there beyond the climatology; well below 1 it still narrows the outcome. It is not a comparison with the ensemble's usual spread at the same lead time, which rises toward 1 with the lead whatever the weather. Over ground above 1,500 m the pressure has no ratio (the reanalysis reduces pressure to sea level its own way), and 2 m temperature has no such normal yet, so it shows the spread only. Change: whether this run is more or less certain than the same ensemble's run 6 to 48 hours earlier (the run picked with the “vs … h earlier” buttons), over the same calendar days. A newer run is nearer those days, so its spread is smaller nearly everywhere, by an amount that depends on the lead time and the latitude; the map therefore measures each point's ratio of the two runs' spreads against the typical ratio at the same latitude between the same two runs (the median within 10° of latitude), in per cent. Red: the spread shrank less than is typical there, or grew (this run is relatively less certain); blue: it shrank more. A point is coloured only where an F-test of the two runs' members against that typical ratio is significant, with the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure over the whole map at a false discovery rate of 10 %, and left blank elsewhere: with 20 to 50 members the ratio of two standard deviations carries 15 to 20 % of sampling noise at every point, so an uncoloured map means the run's spread changed no more than that noise; the hover gives the plain change in the field's units too. Measured this way, a change that covers a whole latitude band is folded into the typical one, so the map shows where the change is regional. The test assumes roughly normally distributed members, which holds better for 500 hPa height and sea-level pressure than for 2 m temperature under shallow cold air, and with 21 members (GEPS) it seldom has the power to call a change significant. The super-ensemble has no spread maps.

Weather regimes

Each run of each ensemble sorts every member's 500 hPa height on every day into one of four recurring weather regimes of two sectors, the Euro-Atlantic (20–80°N, 90°W–30°E) and North America and the Pacific (20–80°N, 170–30°W), or into none. These are the regimes of the site's multi-model regime view on the extended-range page, classified the same way: four patterns per sector and season from NCEP/NCAR reanalysis 1991–2020 (k-means on 5-day mean height anomalies), one set for October–March and one for April–September, every day taking the set of its own season; a member's 5-day running mean anomaly (against ERA5 1991–2020) goes to the nearest pattern, or to “no regime” when its pattern correlation with that pattern is below 0.25. No model here has a reforecast, so each ensemble's drift is learned from its own past runs: the ensemble-mean anomaly at each lead day, less what the run started on that valid day showed on its first day, averaged over the runs and removed (shrunk towards zero by n / (n + 10)) once ten runs verify a lead.

The timeline stacks, for each ensemble and valid date, the share of its members in each regime, every ensemble on the same dates (their newest runs can start 12 hours apart; each row gives its run) and every regime in the same colour and place, as on the extended-range page. A share is drawn pale unless it differs from that regime's 1991–2020 frequency within 15 days of the date: the 95 % interval of their difference, from resampling the members and the reanalysis years 1,000 times, must exclude zero. The four-ensemble row averages the shares of the ensembles that hold the date with equal weight per ensemble, so the 50 and 51-member ensembles count no more than GEPS's 21; it is pale unless the mean differs from the normal by more than 1.96 standard errors (each ensemble's sampling error over its members, and the normal's over the reanalysis years). A black tick above it marks a date on which the ensembles' most frequent regime is not the same.

The scenarios are the regimes that hold at least six members of the chosen ensemble on the period's middle day (a period's label is its own 5-day mean) or on the day: the share of that regime in every ensemble on that date, side by side with the normal as a tick, and the mean anomaly of just its members, shaded everywhere and dotted where at least k of them agree on its sign (the sign test at 5 % on that regime's member count; fewer than six members can never pass it). Since the previous run: each regime's share against the same ensemble's run 12 hours earlier on the same dates, coloured where Fisher's exact test on the two runs' member counts is significant with the false discovery rate held at 10 % over the table. Each run's labels are published in the multi-model view's format (assets/regimes/data/ensmaps_members_<model>.json).

Clusters

The members of all four ensembles' newest runs are pooled on the same calendar days and grouped into the scenarios their spread supports, for days 3–5, 6–10 and 11–15 (each the mean over its days, counted from the newest run's first full day and cut to the days every run holds) and for each day on its own (its daily mean, from the ensembles that reach it: GEFS and GEPS alone hold day 16), over the Euro-Atlantic, North America and the Pacific (the regimes' boxes, outlined on the maps) and the whole hemisphere north of 20°N. Each member's 500 hPa height and sea-level pressure anomalies, on a 2.5° grid, are divided by their typical 1991–2020 spread over the domain for that time of year and period, weighted by the square root of the cosine of latitude and joined; each ensemble's members together carry a quarter of the weight, so the 50 and 51-member ensembles count no more than GEPS's 21. The leading patterns of the members' differences from the four-ensemble mean (enough to explain 80 % of them) are grouped by weighted k-means (100 starts) into 1 to 5 clusters. More than one is shown only when the split cuts the spread by more than it does in 95 % of 100 synthetic ensembles with the same spread but no clusters, every cluster holds at least 5 % of the weight, and resampling the members gives the same number of clusters at least 60 % of the time; otherwise the page shows one scenario, the four-ensemble mean.

Each scenario's probability is its share of the weight, with the share of each ensemble's own members beside it; its nearest regime is the regime whose pattern correlates best with the scenario's mean 500 hPa anomaly in each sector (“no regime” below 0.25). On the maps the anomaly is shaded where the scenario's members agree on its sign (the sign test at 5 %), the lines are the scenario's mean height (every 6 dam) and pressure (every 4 hPa), and dots mark where the scenario's mean differs from the four-ensemble mean (500 random sets of as many members, false discovery rate 10 %). A split that passes those tests can still be the models' own differences rather than the weather's: on the first days each ensemble's members sit close to their own mean, and the means differ. So every split is also tested against the ensembles the members come from (a permutation chi-square test of ensemble against cluster, 2000 permutations); where it fails at 5 % the page shows no scenarios, says that the ensembles differ and shows each ensemble's own mean instead (marked ≠ in the chart by day). Scenarios are shown only for the windows and days that pass both. Once a model's lead-dependent drift is removed (from ten verified 00 UTC runs per lead on, as for the regimes), the clustering and this test run on the corrected fields. The table gives how the members move from one window's scenarios to the next.

A scenario keeps its letter while its members stay together. Each day's scenarios are matched to the last earlier day's with more than one (the assignment that keeps the most members' weight in place); a scenario takes the letter of the one it is matched to when more than half of its members were in it, a scenario that re-forms after a day of one takes its old letter back, and a new letter appears only when a scenario splits off. The windows are lettered the same way among themselves, so a window's A and a day's A need not be the same scenario. In the chart of scenarios by day each bar is a day's scenarios (grey and marked 1 where the tests find one scenario), and the bands carry the members from one day's scenarios to the next day's.

How the page works

Nothing on this page is a picture. Each model, field and period is shipped as the ensemble's own numbers (the mean anomaly to 0.25 °C, 4 m or 0.4 hPa, the number of members agreeing, and the ensemble mean), on its grid, as a lossless WebP image, and your browser colours, gates, contours and reads it. The 0.1° maps come in 36 tiles of 30° by 60°, loaded as you zoom in; the world view draws every fifth point of them.
