Each ensemble is shown on its own published grid, untouched: the finer grids are not smoothed and the coarser ones are not interpolated before the colours (your browser interpolates between grid points as it draws). Each ensemble shows its newest run; the four can be a run apart, and the date line under the buttons always gives the calendar days a map covers.

Days and periods

A day is a calendar day in UTC, the mean of the 06, 12, 18 and 24 UTC fields. A 00 UTC run has 15 full days (day 1 is the day it starts), a 12 UTC run 14 (day 1 is the next day; its first and last half-days are left out). The periods are days 1–5, 6–10 and 11–15 (11–14 for a 12 UTC run), each the mean of its days. A member that misses any field of a period's days is left out of that period, and a period from fewer than 80 % of the ensemble's members is not drawn.

Anomaly

Each member's mean less the 1991–2020 normal for the same days, and the ensemble mean of those anomalies, coloured only where at least k of the n members give the anomaly the same sign as their mean: the smallest number a two-sided sign test against an even split passes at 5 % (33 of 50 or 51, 22 of 31, 16 of 21). Elsewhere the members are split and the map is grey; the hover still says how many agree.

The normals. 2 m temperature: ERA5-Land over land and ERA5 over the sea (ERA5 interpolated from its sea points only, so a coastal mountain does not chill the water beside it), the monthly means at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC, so the normal samples the daily cycle as the forecast day does, on the 0.1° grid of the IFS, whose orography ERA5-Land shares. On the 0.25° and 0.5° grids the normal is the 0.1° one averaged over each cell and moved by 6.5 °C per kilometre from the IFS's height to the model's own, so a valley or a mountain cell is compared with a normal at its own height. The anomalies are the models' raw output against the reanalysis: they include the warming since 1991–2020 (in the 500 hPa height, a positive anomaly across the tropics) and each model's own biases, which no drift correction removes. 500 hPa height and sea-level pressure: ERA5 at 1.5° (the daily means of the four synoptic hours), interpolated to each grid. Each normal is a smooth curve through the year (the mean and three annual harmonics, fitted so that its month averages match the twelve monthly normals), read at each day. The sea-level pressure anomaly is not drawn where the model's ground is above 1,500 m: there the pressure “at sea level” is an extrapolation that each model and the reanalysis make differently.

Change from the previous run

This run's ensemble mean less the previous run's (12 hours earlier) over the same calendar days, on a common 0.5° grid (each member's field averaged over the 0.5° cells first). It is coloured only where the change is significant: Welch's t-test on the two runs' members at every point, and the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure over the whole map at a false discovery rate of 10 %, so that among the coloured points at most a tenth are expected to be chance. The last period of a 00 UTC run is compared over days 11–14, the days the previous 12 UTC run holds. Where the ensembles are confident (the first days) even a small change can be significant: read the size from the colours.

How the page works

Nothing on this page is a picture. Each model, field and period is shipped as the ensemble's own numbers (the mean anomaly to 0.25 °C, 4 m or 0.4 hPa, the number of members agreeing, and the ensemble mean), on its grid, as a lossless WebP image, and your browser colours, gates, contours and reads it. The 0.1° maps come in 36 tiles of 30° by 60°, loaded as you zoom in; the world view draws every fifth point of them.
