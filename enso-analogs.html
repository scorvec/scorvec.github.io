Atmospheric Fingerprints: Z500 / MSLP / 2 m Temperature / Precipitation / 100 m Wind

Monthly circulation-anomaly maps for the current event beside the five super El Niños of the record — every event whose peak RONI exceeded +2.0 (1972–73, 1982–83, 1991–92, 1997–98, 2015–16), the same set the seasonal composite below averages. Pick a variable, a region — the Pacific–North America sector or South & Central America — and any month of the ENSO cycle (June of the development year through May of the following year). Anomalies are trend-adjusted: a per-gridpoint linear fit per calendar month (1950–present) is removed, so a 1982 panel and a 2026 panel are each measured against their own era's climate — what remains is the ENSO signal (the deepened Aleutian low, the PNA arc, coastal Ecuador/Peru rains, the Amazon/Colombia dry signal), not background warming. On the Pacific–NA maps the legend in each panel gives that month's mean teleconnection indices — PNA, NAO and AO from CPC's official monthly values, EPO computed from the same ERA5 heights (Riddle et al. 2013 boxes) — so the map pattern and the index summary can be read together.

Super-El Niño Composite: Seasonal Means with Significance

The average anomaly pattern across every El Niño in the record whose peak RONI exceeded +2.0 — the "super" events on the relative index — by 3-month season of the ENSO cycle. Pick a variable, region and season (JJA of the development year through the following MAM). Each map is the composite mean of the trend-adjusted anomalies (per-gridpoint 1950–present fit per month, so events from the 1970s and 2010s are on equal footing); stippling marks where the composite differs significantly from zero (two-tailed one-sample t-test at 95%), i.e. where the events agree strongly enough to trust the signal rather than chance. The panel footer lists the events that went into the composite — this is the "expected" super-event fingerprint to read the current event against.

ERA5 monthly means (CDS) · composite of peak-RONI>2 events · trend-adjusted anomalies · stippling: 95% two-tailed t-test

Subsurface Cross-Section vs. Analogs

Equatorial depth×longitude temperature anomaly at the same phase (~the latest data week) of each event.
