MJO with the El Niño signal removed

Since 2014 the standard RMM no longer removes ENSO; in a strong El Niño its standing convection projects onto phases 7–8 and masquerades as a stalled MJO. This is the same AIFS-ENS forecast (wind-only), observed track and IFS overlay with the part tied to the Niño-3.4 change removed. Latest init: 2026-09-27 00Z.

No coherent MJO in the ENSO-removed forecast: ensemble-mean amplitude 0.8 (max 1.0) and a phase speed of -5.3°/day, where a real MJO moves at about +7°/day. NOAA’s OLR-based ROMI on 2026-09-23: amplitude 0.92 (phase 5); over the last 60 days 7 % of days reached amplitude 1. Correction applied: ΔNiño-3.4 = +0.94 K.

How the El Niño signal is removed

Wheeler & Hendon (2004) removed two things before projecting onto the MJO patterns: the part of each day’s anomaly linearly related to ENSO, then the mean of the previous 120 days. The Bureau of Meteorology did both until the end of 2013; since 2014 it removes only the 120-day mean, as this page’s raw index does. While an El Niño is growing, the 120-day mean trails the warming, so the leftover projects onto the MJO phases.

Here the leftover is taken out directly: the wind-only RMM minus ΔN34 × c, where ΔN34 is Niño-3.4 (NOAA OISST, 31-day mean) minus its mean over the same 120-day window, and c is the regression of the 120-day-filtered 850 and 200 hPa equatorial winds on ΔN34, longitude by longitude, projected onto the MJO patterns. It is fitted on ERA5 (ARCO) 2003–2024 with ERSST v6 Niño-3.4 (BoM used its own SST index, SST1). Against BoM’s ENSO-removed RMM (2003–2013, 3,899 days) the cleaned wind-only index correlates 0.96/0.96 (RMM1/RMM2), against 0.94/0.96 before, and its correlation with the Niño-3.4 change falls from -0.21 to -0.01. While an El Niño grows, it lowers the share of “active” days: JAS 2015 0.33 → 0.29; JAS 2023 0.38 → 0.29; JAS 2009 0.42 → 0.49. The forecast holds Niño-3.4 at its latest value, since ENSO changes little in 15 days.

The correction is linear, so it removes part of a growing El Niño’s projection, not all of it. The status line therefore also quotes NOAA’s OLR-based real-time OMI (ROMI), which removes the previous 40 days and so is far less sensitive to slow signals: an independent check on whether a real MJO is present. Wind-only (no precipitation channel), and no 12Z cycle offset correction, unlike the raw diagram above.
