Real-time Multivariate MJO (RMM) phase-space forecast from the ECMWF AIFS-ENS ensemble (51 members to day 15), following Wheeler & Hendon (2004). Full three-channel projection: U850/U200 plus a pseudo-OLR channel built from −standardized tropical precipitation (tropical rain and OLR anticorrelate closely); falls back to wind-only if precip is unavailable for a cycle. MJO impacts by phase and month

What each MJO phase has meant for temperature, rainfall and the 500 hPa flow, month by month: the observed record next to 11 CMIP6 models whose MJO passes a realism screen. Only statistically significant cells are coloured; blank means no significant signal.

How this is built

MJO index. Observed: the Bureau of Meteorology’s RMM (Wheeler & Hendon 2004), 1979–2024. CMIP6: the same RMM computed from each run’s own daily OLR and 850/250 hPa zonal wind (the CMIP6 daily archive has no 200 hPa), 15°S–15°N means, the run’s own seasonal cycle and previous 120-day mean removed, projected on the observed W&H EOFs. Both indices are then band-passed to 20–100 days and renormalised. BoM removes the ENSO signal only up to 2013; after that a strong El Niño’s standing pattern projects on RMM as slow “phase 6–8” days (DJF 2015/16 was “active” on 89 % of days), and the CMIP6 index would carry the same artefact. A linear Niño-3.4 regression removed almost none of it, so the band-pass is used for both datasets alike. Check, DJF share of active days after the filter: strong El Niño winters 0.69 (n 4; 0.70 before), neutral 0.62 (n 13); the difference is not significant (Mann–Whitney p 0.69). A day counts when the amplitude is at least 1; the composite is the mean anomaly on the same day or 10 days later, over those days in the chosen month and its two neighbours (a centred three-month window, so “January” uses December–February days: it triples the observed sample). Anomalies are from each dataset’s own seasonal cycle and linear trend. Checks: the same machinery on ERA5 winds reproduces BoM’s RMM1/RMM2 at r 0.94/0.96 (wind-only, 1991-2020); substituting 250 for 200 hPa keeps the phase on 90 % of active days (r 0.99/0.99).

Model screen (fixed before looking at any composite): east/west power ratio of 10°S–10°N rain (wavenumbers 1–3, 30–96 days, November–April) ≥ 2.0 and eastward propagation (lag-regression pattern against GPCP) r ≥ 0.8; GPCP itself gives E/W 2.5. Used: CNRM-ESM2-1 (E/W 3.2, r 0.85), CNRM-CM6-1 (E/W 3.2, r 0.85), CAMS-CSM1-0 (E/W 2.7, r 0.91), CESM2 (E/W 2.5, r 0.91), MPI-ESM1-2-HR (E/W 2.8, r 0.81), GFDL-CM4 (E/W 2.3, r 0.95), EC-Earth3-CC (E/W 2.3, r 0.95), CESM2-FV2 (E/W 2.2, r 0.93), EC-Earth3 (E/W 2.1, r 0.95), IITM-ESM (E/W 2.3, r 0.84), BCC-ESM1 (E/W 2.2, r 0.82). Passed but left out as a near-duplicate of a family member: CNRM-CM6-1-HR, EC-Earth3-Veg, EC-Earth3-Veg-LR, CESM2-WACCM. Failed: EC-Earth3-AerChem (E/W 1.9, r 0.94), MPI-ESM1-2-LR (E/W 2.3, r 0.75), MPI-ESM-1-2-HAM (E/W 2.3, r 0.69), TaiESM1 (E/W 1.7, r 0.90), KACE-1-0-G (E/W 1.6, r 0.85), CMCC-ESM2 (E/W 1.5, r 0.90), AWI-ESM-1-1-LR (E/W 1.8, r 0.78), HadGEM3-GC31-MM (E/W 1.6, r 0.87), IPSL-CM5A2-INCA (E/W 1.7, r 0.76), ACCESS-ESM1-5 (E/W 1.4, r 0.84), CMCC-CM2-HR4 (E/W 1.4, r 0.87), CMCC-CM2-SR5 (E/W 1.4, r 0.89), IPSL-CM6A-LR (E/W 1.4, r 0.85), ACCESS-CM2 (E/W 1.3, r 0.86), BCC-CSM2-MR (E/W 1.5, r 0.74), HadGEM3-GC31-LL (E/W 1.2, r 0.88), UKESM1-0-LL (E/W 1.1, r 0.85), CanESM5 (E/W 0.9, r 0.76), INM-CM5-0 (E/W 1.0, r 0.67), INM-CM4-8 (E/W 0.8, r 0.68). 18 historical members in all (up to two per model), 1979–2014; the 500 hPa composites use each model’s first member.

Tests. CMIP6: each model’s composite (members pooled; at least 30 active days), across-model one-sample t-test with a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery rate of 10 % over the map, and at least 80 % of the models agreeing on the sign. Observed: an MJO passage lasts days and daily anomalies are correlated, so the unit is the event (a run of consecutive active days in one phase): an event-block bootstrap of the composite, the same 10 % false-discovery rate, and only phase-months with at least 8 events are tested.

CMIP6 historical daily fields via the Pangeo cloud archive (anonymous GCS) · ERA5 2 m temperature and 500 hPa height (Copernicus C3S, 1979–2024) · GPCP 1DD v1.3 daily precipitation (NOAA NCEI CDR, 1997–2024) · RMM index: Australian Bureau of Meteorology · static product, redrawn only when the analysis changes.
